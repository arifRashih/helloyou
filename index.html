A runner
made,
not born.

I started running in August 2023 — not for a race, not for a goal, just to see if I could. The first few kilometres were humbling. I was clocking 10:00/km and every run felt like an argument with my own body.

I kept going anyway. Week by week, the pace dropped. The distance grew. Three years later, I've run four full marathons and a sub-4:30 finish. Not elite numbers — but mine, earned on early mornings before the city wakes up.

I run because it makes me sharper. Better husband, better father, more focused at work.

It doesn't take from the other parts of life — it funds them.

Rise and
Shine.

Running alone teaches you discipline. Running with RSC taught me that it doesn't have to be lonely. There's something about moving with a crew — the accountability, the banter, the shared exhaustion — that changes what running means to you.

These are my people. Early mornings, all conditions, no excuses.

How I train.
How I think.

01

Consistency
over intensity

Showing up on the bad days matters more than crushing the good ones. The body adapts to repetition, not heroism. One skipped week costs more than a slow kilometre.

02

Recovery
is training

Sleep is the session most people skip. Every hard run is a withdrawal — rest is the deposit. I stopped treating recovery as a reward. It's part of the plan.

03

Run for the
long life

Not the next race. Not the next PB. Running at 40 is an investment in 60. The goal isn't to go fast — it's to still be going, decades from now.
